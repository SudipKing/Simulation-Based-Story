#B BOOK FIVE|The Age of Tears|Years 407 to 582

#C Chapter Fourteen: The Last of the Humans

#E Humanity was the shortest-lived of all the peoples of the world, and perhaps for that reason the most brightly burning.

For sixty-six years the world was quiet, and the quiet was the loneliest sound it had ever made.

In Anaconda, the old jungle capital, the humans grew in number for a time. A few married, and a few more were born, and for a season the narrow streets echoed again with the cries of children and the haggling of the market. But their lives were brief, and the span of one human was less than a dwarf's long afternoon. Grih's ruling councils watched them with a curious, detached sympathy, as a gardener watches the cycles of the quick-blooming flowers. Soon, one by one, the humans began to perish: of fever, of age, of the simple fact of being human.

In the Year 472 the last of them, a woman named Aria, stood in the empty marketplace of Anaconda and declared a new empire. She called it Ense. It was a nation of one. She was old, and her hair was white as the old snow, and she carried the weight of five hundred years of a people she had never met. Her coronation was a short one. A month later, in a small room under a window that looked out on the jungle where her ancestors had once hunted, she closed her eyes, and the humans were gone from the world.

There was no great funeral. There was no great song. A few dwarves in the garrison laid flowers on her grave and then went back to their posts.

In Nile, four elves remained, fishing the river as they always had. In seventy years, only two new elves had been born there. The dwarves of Metallica, who now numbered more than two thousand, looked at that small village and decided, with the kind of indifference that is the most dangerous mercy, that it was not worth the trouble to destroy. King Grih, rapt in his old triumphs, let it stand.

It would prove the most costly decision of his long life.

#C Chapter Fifteen: The Storm That Broke the World

#E In the Year 473, the sky began to weep, and it did not stop.

It began with thunder, and thunder is nothing. But the thunder did not pass. It rolled over the continent for days, a continuous dull roaring like a beast with the whole world in its jaws, and then the rain began. It was not the rain of the old jungle, warm and generous. It was cold and heavy and falling in sheets, hour after hour, week after week, until the ground itself could not drink any more.

The Age of Tears had come.

The great storm, a hurricane the size of a nation, hung over the heart of the world, and in its eye a terrible thing happened. The continent, which had been one unbroken sheet of land since the first sunrise, began to split. The mountains that had once stood between elves and men, destroyed in the Year 120 and turned to desert, had left the center of the land weak and sandy. The water found it. The storm carved a channel through the world, diagonally, from one corner of the ocean to the other, and the ocean rushed in.

When the thunder at last died, the world was in two halves.

On the left, to the north and the west, stood the dwarves: The Rock, The Stone, O'Raka, the cities of Metallica, all on a single stretch of land. On the right, to the south and the east, stranded across the new water, were Nile, with its four elves, and Anaconda, with its three dwarven garrison. The two shores were separated by a tide of cold green water that no one had ever crossed.

And the storm was not done. The enchantments of the old woods, the silver-barked trees, the blue-lit glades, the moss that glowed at midnight, were pulled out of the soil like teeth. The jungle, which had stood for four centuries, turned to a mire. The murky waters stank of rot. The mud was thick as porridge. The land, which had once been the loveliest in the world, was now a swamp.

Dwarves are creatures of stone. They love dry ground and deep tunnels, and a marsh is hateful to them. The three dwarves in Anaconda, left behind on the wrong side of the world, huddled in their wet fort, knowing they were probably dead. And in Nile, where four elves had been quietly ignored for seventy years, something began to stir.

Grih, in the Rock, understood too late what his mercy had cost him. Metallica could not cross the water. The elves, whom he had let live, might do whatever they chose.

#C Chapter Sixteen: Embera's Rebellion

#E "A kingdom is not made of towers. A kingdom is made of those who refuse to leave."

Her name was Embera, and she was the leader of Nile, a small, unremarkable woman with fishing-rough hands and a gaze like a drawn bow.

She stood on the bank of the new river in the Year 491, eighteen years after the great storm, and looked across the water at the dwarven mainland. She had spent those eighteen years watching, waiting, counting the dwarven patrols that never came. And at last, with her four fellow elves gathered behind her on the muddy shore, she did what the ghost of King Sylvas would have wished.

She declared a nation. She called it Aethel.

It was a different thing from Aethelgard. It was smaller and meaner, born not in the glory of the Enchanted Wood but in the wet misery of the swamp. But it had a flag, and a name, and a will. And when two elves who had been living in the dwarven city of Yeth heard of it, they crossed the river on a raft and joined her. The whole city of Yeth, in a gesture that no one could have foreseen, followed them. A city of dwarves proclaimed itself part of Aethel, in the name of liberty from an empire that had ruled them for ninety years.

Grih did not waste time on speeches. He declared war.

The people of Yeth did not back down, though they had no army, and no walls, and nothing but the rags on their backs. The old king's soldiers came across the narrow part of the water and fell upon them. There was no surrender, and so there was annihilation. Two hundred dwarves of Yeth, men and women and children, were slaughtered in their homes, and the city that Grih had built in honor of a dead king was razed to its foundations.

It was a horror, and the dwarves of Metallica knew it. The loss took a heavy toll on the empire's heart.

And there was no time to mourn, for nature itself was turning against them. In the Year 491, in the center of The Rock, the plants began to move.

It started with a single vine in the royal garden. It climbed the wall, found a window, and strangled a gardener in his sleep. Then the roots of the trees in the courtyard burst the pavement, and the creepers came down from the hills, and the flowers of the mountain meadows bared thorns that no flower had ever grown. Fifty dwarves died in a single night, torn by thorns and wrapped in leaf.

The dwarves fought the plants, and the plants fought the dwarves, and at the same time the war with Aethel ground on. But neither side could advance. The dwarves could not cross the water. The elves could not hold it. The river was a wall as certain as the mountain had been.

And in Anaconda, in the swamp that was no home for any dwarf, something happened that no one expected. Two children were born.

They were small and pale, and they coughed in the damp. But they lived.

#C Chapter Seventeen: Boats, Bog, and Ancient Ruins

#E The war ended in the Year 502 because there was nothing left for anyone to do.

The two sides, exhausted by a conflict neither could win, simply stopped. There was no treaty, no parley, no ceremony. The soldiers of Metallica laid down their spears on the western shore. The elves of Aethel lowered their bows on the east. Across the river, for the first time in eleven years, no one was shooting at anyone.

The world went on, and progress was made.

In Aethel, the elves, a few at first and then a few more, began to master the one craft that the water gave them. They were an old people, and they remembered how to listen to timber. By the Year 552 they had grown to twelve, a small, stubborn number, but growth is growth. They had built boats: small fishing craft, with sails of woven reed, tough little vessels that rode low in the swamp-water. They were not warships yet. But those who had eyes to see could already tell what they would become.

Anaconda, too, was twelve. No one understood how. The land, a seething swamp of mosquitoes and rot, was uninhabitable for any dwarf. The garrison who had been stranded there had grown into a community, and the children of the two born in the war had grown, and the swamp-born dwarves had adapted to their strange home, with webbed hands and leather lungs and a loathing for the stone halls of their ancestors.

Metallica, meanwhile, was reaching for a peak it had nearly touched a century before. It had almost reached two thousand, a hundred years earlier, only to be dashed by the rebellion and the living plants. Now it crossed the limit. By the Year 552 the dwarves of the mainland numbered two thousand and a hundred, the highest of their long history. They did a thing that frightened the elves, who watched from across the water with their long memories. They removed the ruins.

All of them. Every broken wall of Anaconda's old human quarter. Every cracked pillar of Nile. They scoured the mainland of its past, tearing up the stone, filling in the foundations, leaving only the new. It was as if, in the dwarven mind, history was a kind of dirt. Yeth, the city that Grih had built in the name of the old king and then destroyed with fire, was erased so completely that a stranger would never have known it existed. O'Raka, the old northern village, expanded its borders to cover the place where it had stood.

It was an eerie thing, the way the dwarves forgot. It was as if Yeth had never been.

#C Chapter Eighteen: The Crocodile Kingdom

#E In the Year 555, something woke in the swamp.

The crocodiles had lived in the marshes since before the first sunrise. They were old, cold-blooded, patient creatures, the oldest living things on the continent, and for a thousand generations they had done nothing but lie in the mud with their eyes just above the surface of the water, waiting for something to happen.

Something did. Perhaps it was the swamp itself, thick with the stagnant magic of the storm that had torn the Enchanted Wood apart. Perhaps it was the gaze of the Watchers, who had grown fond of strange experiments. In any event, in the Year 555, the crocodiles of the great swamp mutated.

They stood. They found that they had hands. They found that they had words.

Within a single generation, they were building. Not with the grace of the elves or the stubbornness of the dwarves, but with a patient, brutal efficiency. They raised mud-brick halls and woven-reed longhouses. They developed songs, ancient and low, that vibrated in the water. They had a culture of their own, and a code, and a flag of green-black stripes.

The greatest of them was named Ali. He was fifteen feet long and heavy as a boulder, with a gaze that could stop a charging boar. He was proclaimed the first king of Ali Land, and he raised its capital in the center of the marsh and called it Ali City. He ruled fifteen others.

Fifteen. In a world where elves counted twelve and dwarves of the swamp counted twelve, fifteen crocodiles was a respectable nation.

And so, for the first time since the dwarves had crossed the mountain, a new civilization had entered the world. It was a civilization of hunters, of carnivores, of beings whose ancestors had eaten anything that moved. The elves and the dwarves, who watched from their respective shores, did not know what the crocodiles would do when they discovered the other peoples of the world. Would they live in harmony? Or would the old primal hunger rise, and eat them all?

It was a question that would take a long time to answer.

#C Chapter Nineteen: The Return of the Pumpkins

#E "We are bad at farming. We are very, very bad at it."
(Attributed to King Ali of Ali Land, shortly before his death)

In the Year 572, the pumpkins returned.

Ali, who had tried to teach his people the arts of agriculture, was not a good farmer. Nor were any of his subjects. They planted seeds in the wrong soil and watered them with the wrong water, and they turned the earth with the claws of hunters rather than the patience of growers. The crops sprouted, bent, and twisted. They grew in ugly knots, black-veined and swollen. And one grey morning, the fields of Ali Land came alive.

The monsters rose out of the earth in a screaming tide, great orange horrors with eyes of burning pith. Ali and his fifteen crocodiles fought them with tooth and claw, but pumpkins do not bleed. Ali was struck down in the field he himself had planted. The great king of the crocodiles died with his jaw clamped on a stem, and his people, six in number, fled to the center of the swamp, led by a crocodile named Azer, who took the throne.

And the pumpkins, which would not be stopped, rolled on.

It was then that a stroke of luck, strange and almost comic, saved the swamp. A tornado, an enormous black funnel, dropped out of the clouds and picked up the horde of pumpkins like a child gathering toys. It carried them into the sky, over the river, over the water, and dropped them on the far shore, in the half-colonized jungle on the dwarven mainland.

The pumpkins fell on Metallica like a rain of fruit.

The dwarves of The Stone marched out to meet them. It was a terrible battle. Each monstrous pumpkin was worth four dwarves in strength, and the soldiers of Metallica, who had never fought anything like them, died in the dozens. After a month, they won, but the price was over a hundred dwarves. The jungle fringe was covered in orange gore.

In Aethel, meanwhile, the elves had improved their boats and were stuck at fourteen. Anaconda was growing. And the river, between all the nations, kept flowing.

#C Chapter Twenty: The Azer Legion

#E In the Year 582, six peoples sat at the edge of the world, and a seventh was about to be born.

Anaconda had risen to twenty-eight. Aethel had fifteen. Ali Land, to everyone's delight, had risen to seventeen: the crocodiles, whatever else could be said of their farming, were good at breeding.

Metallica had fallen back to nineteen hundred and ninety. It had never fully recovered from the plants, the pumpkins, and the sweeping loss of Yeth. In defiance of that sorrow, Grih did a thing that made the soldiers of the Rock fall silent. He built a new city and called it Yethigrih.

Yeth, and Grih. The dead king and the living one, joined in a single name. The people of Metallica said nothing. But all of them, to a dwarf, wondered the same thing, whether the old king was finally old enough to feel the weight of everything he had done.

And in Nile, something remarkable had happened. The ruler of the elves was no longer a stranger. The old leader Embera had passed away, and her daughter had taken the crown. Her name was Ember.

Queen Ember was a different kind of ruler. She was not a warrior, though she could fight. She was not a priestess, though the trees listened to her. She was a patient, clever woman, with a long, sharp gaze, and the habit of making a plan and then saying nothing for years. At her side, a very old elf, ancient beyond memory, who had somehow survived every war and every winter since the fall of Sylvanor. He was Tohil. The traitor. The kin-slayer. He had become, by some strange alchemy of time, a faded advisor to the new queen, and the elves, who had long since lost the strength to hate, let him be.

In the Year 586, Queen Ember sent a messenger to Azer, king of the crocodiles, with a gift of fine boats and a hand of alliance.

Azer accepted. He had only one condition: that the alliance carry his name. So it was done. The elves and the crocodiles, two unlikely peoples with nothing in common but a swamp and a shared fear of the dwarves, joined in an alliance called the Azer Legion.

No one knew why Ember had made it. But she was a patient queen, and she had learned, from the long history of her people, that the best time to strike was never the first.

#B BOOK SIX|The Age of Sails and Fire|Years 591 to 676

#C Chapter Twenty-One: Fire-Staff and Fever

#E The pumpkins did not forget, and neither did the earth.

In the Year 591, the pumpkins came again, this time from the soil in the center of The Stone, and sixty-five dwarves died in their beds. In the Year 595, they came to Ali City, where Azer, the survivor king, was killed. Only two crocodiles remained alive in the whole of Ali Land. One of them was Dhil, a soldier, who found himself, somewhat to his dismay, the king.

It was a short reign, and would have been shorter. As the pumpkins surrounded the last of the crocodile hall, a warrior stepped out of the mist. He was a dwarf, the very last thing the crocodiles had expected, and in his hand was a staff that burned with a pale, flickering fire.

His name was Aron.

Aron was a soldier of The Rock, and nobody could explain how he had come to be there. No ships had yet crossed the swamp. No bridges spanned the river. And yet there he stood in the middle of Ali City, a dwarf in a crocodile's palace, with a fire-staff he had taken from a mage whom he had killed in some forgotten corner of the world. With a flick of his wrist, he set the pumpkins aflame. A burst of orange fire rolled over the field. When it cleared, twenty-three monsters lay in ashes.

He saved the king. And then, having saved him, he did something extraordinary: nothing at all. He sat down in the sun, in the royal courtyard, and went to sleep. He stayed in Ali Land, to the vexation of the crocodiles, a lazy, snoring hero with a fire-staff on his lap. In the sunny afternoons, they learned to step around him.

Meanwhile, in the mainland, an earthquake rocked the center of The Rock and The Stone, killing many. Metallica, which had been climbing toward two thousand, fell to sixteen hundred. Anaconda had risen to fifty-eight, and Nile to eighteen.

In the Year 607, another quake hit The Stone. Only a few died, but many houses were lost. Yethigrih, the coastal pride of the dwarves, built a dock and began to learn the arts of shipwrights. And in Ali Land, the crocodiles, who had been two for twelve years, remained two. Both were male. It explained a good deal.

#C Chapter Twenty-Two: The Warship Fry

#E In the Year 611, the first warship in the history of the world was launched, and it fired its first cannon on a people who had never done it any harm.

She was called Fry. The elves of Aethel built her on the muddy docks of Nile, a ship of black timber and green sails, with weapons that no ship had ever carried: heavy bolt-throwers on the prow, and a fire-pot on the stern. Queen Ember watched her launch from the shore, her face a mask of calm. When the ship was ready, the elves tested her.

They tested her on the people of O'Raka.

From the coast, a safe distance, Fry fired her bolts across the water and into the harbor of the northern village. Five dwarves died. There was no warning, no declaration of war. The dwarves ran for cover and the elves watched them run, and the queen said nothing.

And then the earth shook.

An earthquake struck O'Raka. It ripped down houses and split the harbor wall, and the shock rolled out across the strait and caught the warship Fry in a wave. A hundred dwarves died in the collapse of the village. The ship was destroyed with all hands, and the elven dock itself, the labor of years, was shattered into splinters.

It seemed, then, that the world had its own opinion about warships.

But the story did not end at the water's edge. After the earthquake, something strange and terrible began among the dwarves. No one has ever learned how it started. Some said it was a rumor; some said a quarrel in a tavern; some said that the madness of the Age of Tears had finally seeped into the stone. In The Rock, in The Stone, in O'Raka, dwarves who had lived peacefully side by side for centuries picked up their axes and began to kill one another.

Two hundred dwarves died in the infighting. Brothers killed brothers. Wives killed husbands. The chroniclers, who tried later to understand it, could find no cause, no reason, no spark. It simply happened, as storms happen, and then it stopped.

Loss after loss. The dwarves of Metallica staggered under it.

#C Chapter Twenty-Three: The Cats

#E In the Year 618, a new people opened its eyes in the burning light of the southern jungle.

They were small things at first, only fifteen of them, lean and tawny and silent, on the jungle coast of the dwarven mainland. A few jungle cats, once beasts, who woke one morning with a strange new awareness. They found that they could think. They found that they could speak. They found that, if they sat in a circle and told one another stories, the stories grew.

Their queen was named Cater.

She was a slim, golden cat with a gaze that held the memory of ten thousand hunts. She gathered the others and raised a nation. She called it the Kitten Empire, which made the dwarves of Yethigrih snort when they heard it, and she built a capital called Meow. She was, in those first years, a quiet and clever ruler, more interested in trade than in conquest.

In Aethel, the elves had not forgotten Fry. They rebuilt their dock, and a second beside it, and in the same year launched a new warship, Fry 2, and their numbers crept up to twenty-one. Yet the great old empire was thinning out. Metallica had slipped to eleven hundred, and more than half of its people were over two hundred years old; there were not enough young dwarves to carry the old ones' work forward. And in Ali Land, the last two crocodiles passed their days waiting for their own end.

The dwarves of Yethigrih, meanwhile, were learning to build. They launched a warship of their own and named her Yeth 1. In the Year 625, they launched another, Yeth 2, and the soldiers of The Rock took her out across the swamp, landed on the shore, and walked among the marsh-lands, looking and listening. No fight came of it. They looked, and they went home.

In the Year 630, Aethel, which had grown to twenty-three, built Fry 3, and the cats, who had a harbor of their own, began to trade with the elves. Silk and spice and cunning went back and forth between the shores. Anaconda, meanwhile, had fallen to eighty-three. In the Year 635, a second wave of crocodiles evolved, and with them came the first females of their kind, and the kingdom of Ali Land, long a pair of weary old men, exploded into twelve.

And then, in the Year 641, the great Grih died.

He died in the Rock, in his own bed, at the age of four hundred and ninety-six. It was a long death and a peaceful one. He had ruled for three hundred and forty-eight years, a reign unmatched in all the history of the world. He had been a boy-king of Rakas, a farmer, a hero, a conqueror, a builder, and a father. He had killed no one who did not deserve it, and he had saved many who did not. His people wept in the streets.

His daughter, Gris, took the throne with sixteen hundred dwarves to rule, and her first act was to stand beside her father's body and promise him, in a whisper, that she would finish what he had started.

#C Chapter Twenty-Four: The Gathering Storm

#E In the Year 645, the world became crowded with ships.

The cats built a new city, Cat Town. The crocodiles of Ali Land built their own dock, and a fishing boat to match. O'Raka, which had long been the poor relation of the dwarves, borrowed the shipwright knowledge of Yethigrih and went straight to warships, and the first of its fleet was launched in the Year 645: Perth 1.

In the Year 659, the crocodile king Dhil died, and his sister Raar became queen of Ali Land. She was fierce and clever, and she had a taste for plain speech. She looked at the dwarves across the water, and at the elves, and at the cats, and the alliance that had been signed by Azer so long ago.

In the Year 660, something astonishing happened. The cats, with their new empire and their new fleet, formally joined the elf-crocodile alliance. Three peoples, bound together under the name of the Azer Legion, with their backs to the sea and their faces to the north.

Gris saw it. She was a dwarf of her father's mold, patient and fierce, and she knew what she was seeing: a pact of the small against the large, of the wet against the dry. She knew that sooner or later it would be tested.

She did not wait for it.

In the Year 666, the Year of the Beast, Metallica declared war against the cats.
